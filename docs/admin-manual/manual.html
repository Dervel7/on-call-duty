1. Introduction

What On-Call Duty is, who this manual is for, and the conventions used throughout.

1.1 What On-Call Duty does

On-Call Duty is a scheduling system that builds fair, conflict-free monthly on-call rosters for a hospital's doctors. As an administrator you maintain the list of doctors and their availability and then generate, fine-tune, and publish a duty schedule for any month. The system automatically respects each doctor's monthly duty limit, prevents back-to-back duties, avoids assigning a doctor who is unavailable, and balances weekend workload across the team.

1.2 Who uses the system

There are two roles. This manual covers the administrator role in full.

RoleWhat they do
AdministratorPlans and publishes duty schedules. Manages users, doctors, availability, schedules, and reports. This is the audience of this manual.
DoctorViews their own published duties and personal dashboard, and records their own unavailability (vacation, sick, etc.). Doctors cannot edit schedules.

1.3 The administrator's navigation

When you sign in as an administrator, the top navigation bar gives you everything you need:

Home  Users  Availability  Schedules  Reports  Profile · Logout

Doctors see a smaller menu (Home, My availability, Profile). If a doctor ever sees administrator-only pages, that is a mistake — administrator access is granted only on the Users page.

1.4 Conventions used in this manual

  • New schedule — text styled like a button shows the exact label you click in the application.
  • Bold highlights an important term or the name of a field.
  • Coloured boxes are callouts: Info Tip Caution Important

2. Signing In

How to reach the application, sign in, handle the first-login password, and sign out.

2.1 The sign-in screen

Open the application's web address in a modern browser. You will see the staff sign-in screen.

Enter the Email and Password given to you by whoever set up your account, then select Sign in.

InfoBoth fields are required. The password must be at least 6 characters. If you submit an invalid email or an incorrect password, a red message appears under the form.

2.2 First sign-in: change the initial password

New administrator accounts are created with an initial password equal to the email address. The system's seeded default administrator is admin@oncall.local with password changeme123. On first sign-in, go to Profile and set a private password of your own (see Chapter 9).

Important — securityNever leave an account on its initial/default password. Anyone who knows an email can guess the initial password. Change it before doing anything else, and disable any account that should not exist.

2.3 Sessions and signing out

After signing in you stay signed in as you move between pages, and your session is restored automatically if you reload the browser. To end your session, choose Logout at the top right. Always sign out on a shared computer.

TipIf your account is disabled by another administrator, or if you change your own password, your other active sessions are signed out automatically.

3. The Administrator Dashboard

The Home page is your starting point: a quick health check of any month's schedule.

3.1 Choosing a month

At the top of the dashboard, enter a Year, pick a Month from the drop-down, and choose Apply. The cards and table below update for that month.

3.2 When no schedule exists yet

If a month has not been generated, the dashboard shows a clear empty state and a shortcut to create one.

3.3 Reading a populated dashboard

Once a schedule exists, the dashboard summarises its quality in three parts.

CardWhat it tells you
CoverageHow many of the month's days are filled (e.g. 30 / 30 days filled) and lists any gap days — dates still unassigned. “No gap days” means the month is fully covered.
FairnessThe duty spread (the difference between the busiest and quietest doctor). A spread of 0–1 is labelled Well balanced; anything larger is labelled Imbalanced — review workload. The weekend spread is shown too.
Workload tablePer doctor: number of duties (with a small bar), weekend count, and their monthly Cap. Inactive doctors are flagged.
TipUse the dashboard as a pre-publish sanity check. Aim for “No gap days” and “Well balanced” before publishing a schedule.

4. Managing Users

The Users page manages every login account — administrators and doctors alike — including each doctor's monthly duty cap.

4.1 The users list

Each row is one account, showing Name, Email, Username, Role (administrator or doctor), Status (active or disabled; disabled rows are highlighted), and Actions. Doctor rows also show their Max monthly duties — their personal cap (1–7); administrator rows show — in that column.

4.2 Creating an account

  1. Choose New user.
  2. Fill in Email, First name and Last name.
  3. Pick the Role: administrator (default) or doctor. For a doctor, also set Max monthly duties (between 1 and 7; the default is 7) — the maximum number of on-call duties this doctor can be assigned in any month.
  4. Choose Save. The account is created with an initial password equal to its email.
InfoCreating a doctor creates their login account (role doctor) and profile in one step — the doctor can sign in straight away and record their own availability.

4.3 Editing, disabling, and deleting an account

  • Edit — change name/email and, for doctors, the Max monthly duties cap; changes apply the next time a schedule is generated. The Role is chosen when the account is created and cannot be changed afterwards.
  • Disable / Enable — switch the account on or off. A disabled user cannot sign in, and a disabled doctor is skipped by the scheduling engine. This is reversible and preferred over deleting.
  • Delete — permanently removes the account (you are asked to confirm).
ImportantYou cannot delete a doctor who still has duties in a draft schedule — the system refuses and tells you to disable them instead. Their past duties in published schedules are kept. Disable is reversible; delete is not.

5. Managing Availability

The Availability page records when a doctor is not available (vacation, sick leave, conferences, etc.). The scheduling engine never assigns a doctor on a day they are unavailable.

5.1 The availability list

You can filter by Doctor and by a From / To date range, then choose Apply. The table shows each exclusion's Type, Start, End and optional Note.

5.2 Adding an exclusion

  1. Choose New exclusion.
  2. Select the Doctor.
  3. Choose a Type: Vacation, Sick, Conference, or Other.
  4. Set the Start date and End date (inclusive, whole days; the end must be on or after the start).
  5. (Optional) add a Note (up to 500 characters), then Save.
Caution — overlapsTwo exclusions for the same doctor cannot overlap in time. The system rejects an overlapping record. If you need to extend an absence, edit the existing record instead of adding a second one.
TipDoctors can record their own exclusions from their My availability page. Use this admin page to record exclusions on a doctor's behalf or to correct mistakes.

6. Building a Schedule

This is the core administrative workflow: Preview → resolve any conflicts → Generate → edit → Publish.

6.1 The schedules list

The Schedules page lists every generated month with its Status (Draft or Published), creation date, and a View action. Use the Year filter to narrow the list.

6.2 Step 1 — Preview a month

Before committing anything, preview a month to see whether it can be filled.

  1. Choose New schedule.
  2. Enter the Year and pick the Month.
  3. Choose Preview. The engine runs without saving and reports the result.

6.3 Step 2 — Resolve conflicts if any

If some days cannot be filled, the preview shows a red conflict panel listing each unfillable date and why. Generate stays disabled until conflicts are cleared.

To resolve a conflict, address the cause and preview again:

  • Not enough capacity — a day needs a doctor but too few are eligible. Increase a doctor's Max monthly duties (Users page), add/enable a doctor, or reduce overlapping vacations.
  • Everyone unavailable — adjust the exclusion(s) blocking that date (Availability page).
  • Back-to-back constraint — no doctor is free because each would follow their own previous duty. Add capacity (above) to loosen the chain.
TipEach conflict message reads “of N active doctor(s): X unavailable, Y at cap, Z back-to-back”, so you know exactly what to fix.

6.4 Step 3 — Generate

Once the preview reports no conflicts, choose Generate. The schedule is created in Draft status and you are taken to its detail page. Generating is blocked if a schedule for that month already exists.

6.5 Step 4 — Review and edit the draft (Schedule Detail)

The detail page shows the full month: each date, the assigned doctor (or Unassigned), Flags (Weekend, Gap day), and the Reason the engine chose that doctor (hover the date for the explanation). While the schedule is a draft you can fine-tune it:

  • Edit on a filled day — reassign that duty to another eligible doctor.
  • Remove — clear a duty; the day becomes Unassigned and a + Add button appears.
  • + Add on an empty day — assign a doctor manually.
InfoEvery manual change is re-validated against the same hard rules as automatic generation. If a change would violate a rule (for example a back-to-back duty), it is refused with an explanation.

6.6 Step 5 — Publish (lock) the schedule

When the draft looks right, choose Publish and confirm. The status changes to Published and all editing is locked — duties can no longer be added, reassigned, or removed, and the schedule cannot be deleted.

Tip — need to change a published schedule?Choose Revert to draft to unlock it, make your changes, then publish again.

7. Reports

The Reports page produces a consolidated, printable monthly report — coverage, fairness, the full roster, and per-doctor workload.

7.1 Viewing a monthly report

Enter the Year, pick the Month, and choose Apply. The report is read-only; it cannot be edited here.

SectionContents
Title block“On-Call Duty”, the month, the Published/Draft badge, and the generation timestamp.
CoverageDays filled vs. total, and the list of gap days (if any).
FairnessDuty spread with a Well balanced / Imbalanced badge, plus the weekend spread.
Duty rosterDay-by-day table: date, doctor (or Unassigned), flags, and the assignment reason.
WorkloadPer doctor: duties (with a proportional bar), weekend count, and cap. Inactive doctors are greyed.

7.2 Exporting to CSV

Choose Export CSV to download the month's duty roster as a spreadsheet file named oncall-YYYY-MM.csv. It opens directly in Excel, Google Sheets, or any CSV-aware tool, with columns Date, Weekday, Doctor, Weekend, Reason. The button is disabled when there is no schedule for the selected month.

7.3 Printing or saving as PDF

Choose Print / Save as PDF to open your browser's print dialog. From there you can print a paper copy or choose Save as PDF as a destination to produce a clean, formatted PDF of the report. The on-screen controls are hidden automatically when printing.

TipFor the neatest printed report, set the paper orientation to Portrait and margins to Default in the print dialog.

8. Your Profile

The Profile page is where you change your own password.

  1. Enter your Current password.
  2. Enter a New password (at least 6 characters, and different from the current one).
  3. Choose Update password.
InfoChanging your password signs you out of all your other active sessions. You stay signed in on the current browser.

9. Domain Rules & the Scheduling Engine

This chapter explains the rules the system enforces and how the engine produces a fair roster. You do not need to configure any of this — it helps you understand why a schedule looks the way it does.

9.1 Shifts and on-call timing

Regular weekday shift07:00–15:00.
On-call duty07:00 on the duty day until 15:00 the next day (overnight, handing over at 15:00).
On any dayOn-call duties can fall on any day, including weekends.

9.2 Hard rules (always enforced)

These can never be broken, by automatic generation or by a manual edit:

RuleMeaning
Monthly duty capA doctor cannot exceed their Max monthly duties (1–7, set on the Users page) in a given month.
No back-to-backA doctor cannot be on duty the day immediately after a day they were already on duty. This is checked across month boundaries, so the last day of one month protects the first day of the next.
AvailabilityA doctor is never assigned on a date covered by one of their exclusions (vacation, sick, conference, other).
Active onlyDisabled doctors are skipped entirely.

9.3 How the engine balances work

Among the doctors who satisfy the hard rules for a given day, the engine picks the one with the best score, so that work is spread fairly:

  • Workload — favours doctors with the most remaining headroom under their cap (heaviest weight).
  • Weekend balance — on weekend days, favours doctors who have had fewer weekend duties so far.

Ties are broken deterministically: fewest total duties first, then fewest weekend duties, then lower doctor id. The result is stable — generating the same month with the same inputs always yields the same roster.

9.4 Explainable assignments

Every duty stores a human-readable Reason, visible on the schedule detail page (hover a date) and in the report. It looks like score 18 (workload +12, weekend +6) and notes any tie-break used. Manual changes are recorded as manual override by admin.

9.5 Conflict detection

When a day cannot be filled (no eligible doctor), the preview lists it as a conflict with the exact breakdown (unavailable / at cap / back-to-back). You resolve conflicts as described in §6.3 before generating.

10. Troubleshooting & FAQ

10.1 “Resolve N unfillable day(s) first” when generating

One or more days have no eligible doctor. Open the preview to see which days and why, then: raise a doctor's monthly cap, enable/add a doctor, or shorten a vacation. Preview again until there are no conflicts, then generate.

10.2 “Doctor has duties in a draft schedule”

A doctor with duties in a draft schedule cannot be deleted — finish or discard the draft first, or choose Disable instead: they will be skipped by future schedules and cannot sign in, and you can re-enable them later. Their duties in already published schedules are kept.

10.3 “Schedule is published; revert to draft…”

You tried to edit, reassign, remove, or delete a duty on a published schedule. Choose Revert to draft at the top of the detail page to unlock editing, make your change, then publish again.

10.4 A disabled account cannot sign in

Disabled users are refused at sign-in. Re-enable the account on the Users page if access should be restored.

10.5 I changed a doctor's cap — why didn't the existing schedule change?

Edits to doctors and availability only affect schedules you generate after the change. An existing schedule keeps its assignments. To reflect changes, revert it to draft and adjust manually, or delete and regenerate it.

10.6 The dashboard / report shows an old month

Both pages default to the current month. Enter the Year and Month you want and choose Apply.

10.7 CSV opens with all columns merged into one

Some spreadsheet tools need the comma delimiter stated explicitly. Use the “Import” or “Text to columns” feature and choose Comma as the delimiter.

10.8 The fairness badge says “Imbalanced”

The busiest and quietest doctors differ by more than one duty. This can happen when caps, availability, or the number of days force an uneven spread. Review the workload table and consider adjusting caps or availability, then regenerate.

TermMeaning

DutyA single doctor assigned to a single day (07:00 → next day 15:00).
